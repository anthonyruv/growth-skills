## What you get

An offer brief you can hand to a teammate or use as the starting point for a landing page: a defined buyer, a credible promise, deliverables, a delivery plan, and assumptions to validate.

## When to use it

Use this before writing sales copy, when a package has become hard to explain, or when a list of features is standing in for a customer outcome.

## What to bring

Bring your customer, the problem you solve, what you can actually deliver, your current price if you have one, and any real evidence. Unknowns are welcome; the skill keeps them visible.

## How it works

1. Identify the buying situation and the result the customer wants.
2. Examine outcome, confidence, delay, and effort as design questions.
3. Select the deliverables that address meaningful obstacles within your capacity.
4. Separate supported promises from hypotheses and choose the next validation step.

A stronger promise needs stronger evidence. The skill will not turn a hoped-for outcome into a customer guarantee.

## Try it

> Use create-offer to shape a bookkeeping service for independent design studios. We can reconcile accounts monthly, provide a cash-flow snapshot, and hold a 30-minute review. We have no case studies yet. Draft the offer and flag what we need to validate.

Expect a modest, concrete process promise and a delivery brief—not invented financial results.
