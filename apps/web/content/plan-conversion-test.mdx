## What you get

A test brief with a specific hypothesis, control and challenger, conversion definition, guardrails, and rules for adopting a change, keeping the control, or calling the result inconclusive.

## When to use it

Use it when there are too many possible page changes, when an apparent uplift needs context, or when a team needs to agree on what it will learn before starting an experiment.

## What to bring

Bring the page, audience, conversion event, baseline counts, traffic estimate, and any important quality or revenue constraints. If those numbers are missing, the skill makes measurement the first step instead of inventing a sample size.

## How it works

1. Choose one important buyer uncertainty and write a causal hypothesis.
2. Specify the change, audience, assignment, and measurement.
3. Decide what effect would matter and how uncertainty will be handled.
4. Agree on stopping and decision rules before launch.

A headline and image can be tested together when they form one message. That tells you about the package; it does not tell you which component caused the result.

## Try it

> Use plan-conversion-test for a landing page with 4,000 eligible visits and 120 demo bookings in the last month. We think the headline is too vague. Lead quality matters more than raw booking volume. Draft a test brief and tell us what else we need before choosing a sample size.

Expect a qualified-booking guardrail, an explicit baseline, and questions about the smallest worthwhile improvement and decision risk. Expect no promise that one week of data is enough.
