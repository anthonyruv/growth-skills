# Build a landing page

Create a landing page draft that connects a believable promise to a clear plan, relevant proof, and one primary action. Preserve the user's product and design direction.

## Establish the brief

Use available context to identify the audience, offer, traffic source and awareness, intended action, delivery mechanism, and real evidence. Ask about missing facts that change the promise or CTA. If proceeding with assumptions, list them separately from customer-facing copy.

Clarify what is being sold before writing a headline. Avoid a broad transformation promise when the offer only delivers a narrow tool or process.

## Build the page around the decision

Start with a headline expressing an understandable result for a recognizable buyer, a subheadline explaining what the offer is, and a primary CTA naming what happens next. Use a product view or relevant visual when it helps people understand the offer. Do not default to decorative imagery or claim a layout is guaranteed to convert.

Arrange the remainder for the buyer's actual questions: relevant problem, delivery plan, proof matching the promise, what's included, objections and constraints, and the action. This is a decision framework, not a requirement to include every section on every page.

Tie each feature to an outcome or step in the plan. Keep the strongest useful bullets; each extra point consumes attention. Don't remove details needed for price, terms, suitability, or informed choice merely to shorten the page.

Keep facts separate from placeholders. Never invent reviews, customer logos, results, countdowns, stock limits, or guarantees. Use an explicitly labeled proof gap in the handoff when the proposed claim has no supporting evidence.

## Deliver something usable

Provide the page outline and complete draft copy, including CTA labels and any required form fields. Include a proof inventory mapping claims to supplied evidence, unresolved assumptions, and one first test.

Prioritize a headline or explanatory visual test when the largest uncertainty is first-impression clarity. State why it matters for this audience. Do not promise uplift or prescribe a fixed test duration without traffic and measurement context.

If the user requests implementation, implement in their existing stack and verify core navigation/form behavior. Drafting a page does not by itself authorize publishing, buying ads, or contacting customers.
